# Demand for on-site upholstery cleaning (химчистка мягкой мебели с выездом) in Yerevan, 2025–2026

**How to read these notes (methodology caveat, important for the report writer).**
Research was done on 30 Sep 2026. The sandbox's egress proxy blocked *every* direct page fetch attempted (armstat.am, arka.am, armenpress.am, hetq.am, oc-media.org, jam-news.net, en.wikipedia.org, airdna.co, airbtics.com, bnbcalc.com, spyur.am, list.am, 2gis.am, yandex.com/ru, instagram.com, hvs.com, kommersant.ru, forbes.ru, all provider websites), and the session's web-search budget (200 searches, shared with the other researchers) ran out before the domain-scoped Instagram / Facebook / Telegram / 2GIS / Krasnodar-benchmark queries could run. Consequently **every cited fact below comes from search-engine result snippets and search summaries, not from reading the page**. The URL given is the page the search engine returned for that fact; page-level attribution is "probable" unless stated. Numbers are reproduced exactly as the snippets gave them. Google Trends and Yandex Wordstat could not be queried at all.

Legend used in the findings: **[HARD]** = official statistic (Armstat/Cadastre/industry data provider) as reported in a snippet; **[SOFT]** = claim from a commercial, marketing, crowd-sourced or aggregator page; **[BENCHMARK]** = data from another country, not Yerevan; **[INFERENCE]** = my own calculation or judgement.

## Key Question 1 — Demand drivers with numbers (population, households, income, rentals, relocants, STR/hotels, offices, pets)

### Takeaway
Yerevan is a growing city of ~1.15 million people (Jan 2026), with average nominal wages of ~303k AMD (2025) but a median near ~220k AMD, a large tourist-accommodation base (≈790 hotels and 2.7k–5.8k short-term rental units), and a residual Russian-speaking relocant community officially counted at ~43k residence-permit holders (Dec 2025) with net inflow resuming in 2026. Household counts, apartment share and Yerevan-specific income could not be sourced.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Yerevan grew from 1,093,200 (Apr 2022) to 1,147,600 (Jan 2026), i.e. ≈+54,000 or ≈+5% in under four years — the addressable base is expanding, not shrinking.
- [INFERENCE] With no official household count, a working range can be derived only by assumption: at 3.0–3.5 persons per household, 1.15 million residents ≈ 330,000–380,000 households. Treat as an order-of-magnitude placeholder, not a statistic.
- [INFERENCE] Price points quoted by Yerevan cleaners (3,000–12,000 AMD per item/set; see KQ2) equal roughly 1–4% of the 303k AMD average wage and 1.4–5.5% of the ~220k median — affordable for the middle of the distribution, but a one-off "luxury" for the lower half, which supports a middle-class / relocant / landlord-driven demand profile.
- [INFERENCE] The commercial base that needs periodic upholstery cleaning is large relative to the city: ~790 hotels, 2.7k–5.8k STR units growing ~17% a year, ~100 coworkings — and the STR segment in particular turns over guests weekly, which is where recurring B2B contracts are plausible.
- [INFERENCE] The relocant community is smaller than at its 2022 peak (110k arrivals recorded in 2022 vs 43k permit holders in Dec 2025) but is not vanishing: net +7,000 Russian citizens in Jan–May 2026 and "some 60,000 remaining, mostly in Yerevan" mean a Russian-speaking customer segment that rents furnished flats and is used to ordering cleaning online still exists.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Counting distinct entities in the roster (excluding generic dry-cleaners that mainly do clothes), Yerevan has on the order of **20–25 identifiable providers** of on-site upholstery cleaning, i.e. roughly **2 per 100,000 residents**. Of these, only a handful (Cleantastic, Helpers.am, YVN Clean, Radiance, RT Cleaning, Level Up, Clean House, Rally, SV Cleaning) present themselves as upholstery-first mobile services; the rest are general cleaning firms or dry-cleaners for which sofas are a side line.
- [INFERENCE] Follower counts of 1.6k (YVN Clean) and 4.7k (Helpers.am) are small for a city of 1.15M; no account found has a mass audience, so social-media brand leadership is still open.
- [INFERENCE] Russian-language positioning ("химчистка", "клининг", "Ереван") is used by at least Cleantastic, Helpers.am, YVN Clean, Rally, RT Cleaning and SV Cleaning — evidence that providers consider Russian-speaking customers (relocants and Russian-speaking locals) an important segment.
- [INFERENCE] Price dispersion is wide (2-seater sofa 4,000 AMD at Level Up vs "from 8,000" at RT Cleaning vs "from 12,000" at Clean House Service). ## Key Question 7 — Bottom line: is there enough demand for a new one-person mobile upholstery cleaner, and is the market crowded?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports "unsaturated but thin": the customer base is large and growing (1.15M residents, thousands of STR units, ~790 hotels, a persisting Russian-speaking renter segment), supply is visibly limited (~20–25 providers, small Instagram audiences, no waiting lists, same-day availability), but no direct measure of order volume exists and prices at the low end (4,000–5,000 AMD per sofa) are already being undercut, so income depends on marketing execution rather than on untapped demand alone.

### Cited Findings
- Demand base: Yerevan 1,147,600 residents (1 Jan 2026) [HARD] — [ARKA](https://arka.am/en/news/society/armenia-s-resident-population-increased-in-2025-exceeding-3-09-million); 5,781 STR listings, +17.1% y/y (June 2026) [SOFT] — [AirDNA](https://www.airdna.co/vacation-rental-data/app/am/default/yerevan/supply); 790 hotels in Yerevan (2025) [SOFT] — [HVS](https://www.hvs.com/article/10480-Armenia-Market-Pulse-2026-A-New-Chapter-in-the-South-Caucasus); 43,000 Russian residence-permit holders (Dec 2025) and net +7,000 Russian citizens Jan–May 2026 [HARD] — [OC Media](https://oc-media.org/armenias-population-has-increased-by-84-000-since-2024/), [RFE/RL via GlobalSecurity](https://www.globalsecurity.org/wmd/library/news/russia/2026/08/russia-260821-rferl02.htm)
- Purchasing power: average wage 303,140 AMD (2025), median ≈220,000 AMD [HARD/SOFT] — [ARKA](https://arka.am/en/news/economy/average-monthly-salary-in-armenia-increased-by-5-6-in-2025/), [Armenian-Lawyer.com](https://armenian-lawyer.com/business-immigration/armenia-recruitment-salary-trends-2025/)
- Supply: ≈13 organisations in the Spyur upholstery-cleaning rubric; ~20–25 identifiable providers overall; Instagram leaders at 4,674 and 1,582 followers; same-day / 24/7 availability advertised [SOFT] — [Spyur](https://www.spyur.am/ru/business_directory/bd/24815), [Instagram @helpers.am](https://www.instagram.com/helpers.am/), [Instagram @yvn_clean](https://www.instagram.com/yvn_clean/)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Verdict on saturation: **not crowded** by any observable metric (provider density ~2/100k, small social audiences, immediate availability), but **not proven deep** either — every direct demand proxy (Trends, Wordstat, group requests, review velocity) is missing, so "unsaturated" could equally mean "small".
- [INFERENCE] Who the customers are: middle-income Yerevan families with children/pets; Russian-speaking relocants renting furnished flats (move-in/move-out, allergy/odour concerns, habit of ordering online); landlords and STR hosts between guests/tenants; hostels, small hotels, coworkings and cafés on periodic contracts.
- [INFERENCE] How they find a cleaner: Instagram search/ads and reels, Google/Yandex Maps, Yandex Uslugi and turn.am marketplaces, Spyur, and recommendations in Russian-speaking Facebook/Telegram communities; word-of-mouth from STR managers is the most valuable B2B route.
- [INFERENCE] Realistic entry stance: a solo Russian-speaking operator can plausibly reach 1–3 orders/day within a few months if it (a) prices in the 5,000–9,000 AMD band per sofa with transparent per-item tariffs, (b) targets relocant and STR-host communities where Russian/English service is a differentiator, and (c) collects Google/Yandex reviews aggressively since incumbents have not built large review bases visible in search. The main risk is not lack of demand but rapid copy-cat entry (see Russian benchmark) and the low median income that caps willingness to pay.

- The exchange rate used to relate AMD prices to USD was not sourced in this session; if the report converts prices, it should cite cba.am.
